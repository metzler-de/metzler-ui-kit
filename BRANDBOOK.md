## Introduction
This brandbook translates the Metzler design system into clear rules for every touchpoint — website, print, packaging, and marketing. It is the single source of truth: colors, typography, logo usage, and composition all live here. Use it as a reference when creating campaigns, listing new products, or preparing materials for our print vendors.

#### WHO IT'S FOR
Marketing managers, content editors, product photographers, print vendors, and anyone else representing Metzler externally. No design background required — every rule is written to be followed, not interpreted.

#### HOW TO USE IT
Each chapter covers one system — color, logo, typography, layout. Rules are paired with a visual example and a do / don't comparison. When in doubt, follow the rule rather than your instinct. Consistency is what makes the brand recognisable.

#### WHAT'S INSIDE
Primary color system · Logo variations · Typography hierarchy · Logo construction & proportions · Approved color variants · Incorrect usage reference. All values are sourced from the Metzler design system Figma library.

## Typography

## Logo Versions
The Metzler logotype exists in four approved versions. Red + Black is the default mark used across digital and print. Red + White is the reversed lockup for dark backgrounds and full-bleed brand color fills.

Monochrome white is used for engraving (Gravur) on dark metal or plastic, for embossing on colored stock, and for any print application where only a single light tone is available.

Monochrome black is used for single-color printing — newspapers, fax, stamps, laser engraving on light surfaces — and anywhere the press only renders one ink.

- NOTE
Never place a colored M-square on a colored background and never recolor the wordmark outside the approved set. When in doubt, default to the monochrome black or white version.

## Incorrect Logo Usage
The Metzler logo is never redrawn, recolored, or decorated. Always reproduce it from the approved master file, at the correct proportions, in one of the four approved color variants.

Distorting the lockup — stretching, squashing, rotating, or rearranging the M-square and wordmark — breaks the geometry that gives the mark its balance.

Unapproved colors and effects — tints, gradients, drop shadows, outer glows, outlines — are never permitted, even for decorative layouts.

## Metzler Gradient
Two gradients define the Metzler visual language. Gradient Brand moves from deep teal through near-black — the backbone of hero sections and dark-mode surfaces. Gradient Accent sweeps from Teal Mint to Brand Teal — used sparingly for decorative cards, badges, and highlights. Neither gradient replaces solid brand colors; both complement them.

## Grid & Structure
The skeleton every page hangs on. Set the grid before any design begins, treat facing pages as one canvas, and load the most important content into the zone the reader sees first.

- RULE 01
Build on a 2–3 column grid; think 3×3 for even distribution.

A4 body uses 3 columns with a 4 mm gutter. Overlay a 3×3 matrix and seat each block on a cell so visual weight spreads evenly across the page.

- RULE 02
Design each spread as one unit, not two pages.

Let the hero image cross the gutter and align baselines left-to-right. Keep critical content ≥ 10 mm clear of the spine fold.

- RULE 03
Place key content in the upper third of the page.

Headline, kicker, and lead image sit in the top 33% — the zone read first and visible above a newsstand fold.

## Whitespace & Balance
Space is an active design element, not leftover. Give every element room to breathe, and balance the spread around an optical centre using unequal sizes rather than rigid symmetry.

- RULE 04
Use whitespace deliberately; hold a 2–3 character buffer between image and text.

At 11 pt body that buffer is roughly 5–7 mm. Never let captions or columns touch a picture edge — give every element room to breathe.

- RULE 05
Balance the spread around an optical centre.

One large mass on the left is offset by a smaller accent plus text on the right — unequal sizes, even visual weight. Avoid mirror-symmetry.

## Visual Hierarchy
Guide the eye before a single word is read. Bigger elements read as more important — use size, weight, and colour to rank everything, and give each cover a single dominant focal point.

- RULE 06
Build clear heading levels with size, weight, and colour.

Four steps: H1 28/700, H2 18/700, H3 13/700, body 11/400. Each level differs from the next in at least two attributes.

- № 07
- RULE 07
Strong size contrast; one bold focal point per cover.

The cover line dwarfs everything else — roughly 5–8× the supporting text. A single dominant element, with no competing headlines.

## Typography (Editorial)
Set type for comfortable, scannable reading. Keep the font palette tight, size the body for sustained reading, and break long copy into chunks a skimming reader can navigate.

- DISPLAY · BOLD
- TEXT · SERIF
- RULE 08
Use 1–2 fonts matching the magazine's personality.

Metzler editorial pairs Helvetica Neue for display with one serif text face. Two families maximum — weights do the rest.

- 70 ch
- RULE 09
Body 10–13 pt, leading ~120%, line length 50–70 characters.

House setting: 11 pt / 13.2 pt leading, columns sized to land near 60 characters per line for a steady rhythm.

- RULE 10
Break long copy into scannable chunks; use bullets as entry points.

Cap paragraphs at 3–4 lines. Lead sections with bullets or bold run-ins so a skimming reader still catches every key point.

## Flow & Navigation
Plan the route the eye takes through the spread. Follow the natural reading path, signal any non-linear jumps with clear cues, and reserve the margins for wayfinding marks.

- RULE 12
Guide the reader top-left to bottom-right; cue non-linear jumps.

Follow the natural Z-pattern. When the order breaks, add a numbered marker or arrow so the reader never loses the thread.

- RULE 13
Use the margins for page numbers and markers.

Folios and running heads live in the outer/bottom margin — 8 pt, Graphite 600 — never inside the live text area.

## Consistency
A magazine earns trust by being recognisable. Repeat the same system every issue, and give readers a dependable, consistently formatted table of contents to navigate by.

- RULE 14
Keep the style consistent across all issues.

Logo position, masthead, grid, and folio style are fixed. Only content and the issue number change — three issues read as one family.

- RULE 15
Include a readable, consistently formatted table of contents.

Same TOC layout every issue: section title, dotted leader, page number in tabular figures — aligned and instantly scannable.

## Anatomy of a Cover
A great print cover does one job: stop the reader and sell one story. Every Metzler front — magazine, report, or brochure — is built from the same six zones. Lead with a single idea, give it room, and let the brand hold the frame. Be bold; a cover that plays it safe gets skipped.

##### Masthead
Fixed title and position every issue — the anchor of recognition.

##### Issue & Date
Volume, number, season. Small, consistent, out of the way.

##### Brand Mark
The Metzler M square — clear space kept, never on busy areas.

##### Lead Headline
One dominant cover line. Biggest type on the page by far.

##### Supporting Line
A short deck that earns the headline — kept brief and secondary.

##### Barcode / Folio
Production marks live in the margin, off the focal zone.

## Imagery, Type & Contrast
The three levers that make a cover pop: one striking image, confident typography, and bold contrast between them. Treat each as deliberate — never decorate, always direct the eye.

- TIP 03
Use eye-catching, high-quality imagery.

One hero subject fills the frame at 300 dpi. Place the focal point on a third, leave a calm zone for the masthead and headline.

Typography matters — legible, on-brand, distinctive.

Play with contrast for a bold, creative cover.

## Strategy & Testing
A strong cover is researched, not guessed. Work the process before the polish: know who you're for, see what's already out there, then test and refine the concept with real eyes.

##### Know the Audience
Define who picks this up — their values and pain points. Design for them, not for the brand team.

##### Scan Competitors
Study rival covers on the shelf. Spot the trend, then deliberately stand apart from it.

##### Concept & Season
Tie the lead story to the moment — seasonal colour, theme, or event — and sketch 2–3 directions.

##### A/B Test Layouts
Mock up alternates — image vs type-led, headline placement — and compare engagement.

##### Gather Feedback
Show colleagues and a few readers. Watch the first-glance reaction, then refine.

##### Then be bold.
Research sets the guardrails — it shouldn't sand off the edges. Once the strategy is clear, take a real creative risk on execution; the safe cover is the forgettable one.

## Do / Don't Quick Reference
A single-page scan of the rules that matter most when anyone outside the brand team ships Metzler work. Print it, pin it by the designer's desk. When in doubt, default to the column on the left.

#### Always do
- Use the logo only on white or approved brand backgrounds.
- Maintain the defined clear space around the logo at all times.
- Use approved HEX values — never eyedrop from a photo.
- Scale images proportionally, never stretch or squish.
- Use one typeface family per document — Helvetica Neue.
- Left-align body text in multi-column layouts.
- Use spacing multiples of the 4 px base unit for all padding.
- Deliver print files as CMYK PDF/X-1a with 3 mm bleed.

#### Never do
- Stretch, squish, rotate, or add effects to the logo.
- Place the logo on busy or low-contrast backgrounds.
- Use more than 2 accent colors on a single page.
- Use ALL CAPS for body text or headings longer than 5 words.
- Mix icon libraries — Lucide only, never Material or Font Awesome.
- Center-align text blocks longer than 3 lines.
- Submit RGB or PNG files to print vendors.
- Add drop shadows, glows, or outlines to logos or icons.
